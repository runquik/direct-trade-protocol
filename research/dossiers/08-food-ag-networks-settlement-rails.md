# Food/Ag B2B Trade Networks & the Settlement Rails of Physical Trade

**Era:** 1999–present
**Status:** active (a mixed field: quiet compounding survivors, loud venture corpses, and 90-year-old legal rails that still settle most of the trade)
**One-line:** Two tightly-linked threads — (A) every attempt to digitize food & agriculture B2B trade since the dot-com era, and (B) the trust/settlement machinery (PACA, letters of credit, factoring, platform escrow, Faire's balance sheet, Incoterms) that physical trade actually runs on — examined together because DTP's thesis lives exactly at their intersection.

## 1. Origin Story

### Thread A: the networks

Before 1999, US food wholesale coordination was phone, fax, and the produce industry's own paper institutions: Blue Book credit ratings, terminal-market brokers, PACA reparation dockets. [iTradeNetwork was formed in 1999](https://www.itradenetwork.com/about-us) in the middle of the dot-com B2B exchange bubble (see dossier 04) to move perishables ordering off pen and paper. Nearly every one of its ~1,500 exchange-era contemporaries died; iTrade survived by becoming procurement plumbing for existing trading pairs rather than a spot market for new ones — a distinction this entire dossier keeps re-proving.

Successive waves repeated the attempt with fresh capital: the 2014–2016 ag-fintech wave (ProducePay, FBN, GrubMarket, FarmLead), the 2015–2018 marketplace wave (Full Harvest, Indigo Marketplace, ChefHero, Cheetah, Silo), the 2018–2021 ordering-app wave (Choco, REKKI, Pepper, Cut+Dry, Notch), and the 2011–present compliance wave (ReposiTrak, FoodLogiQ/Trustwell) riding FSMA. Each wave was pitched as disintermediation; the survivors of each wave are, almost without exception, the ones that quietly gave up on disintermediation.

### Thread B: the rails

The settlement rails are far older. The letter of credit — pay against documents proving shipment — is centuries old and was codified by the ICC (UCP, now [UCP 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_credit)). [Incoterms date to 1936](https://www.freightamigo.com/en/blog/logistics/the-history-of-incoterms-from-1936-to-2020-and-beyond/). The US produce industry got its own statutory rail in the [Perishable Agricultural Commodities Act of 1930](https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/paca/paca-trust), amended in 1984 to create the PACA trust after Congress found that produce sellers — shipping goods that rot before disputes resolve — were being wiped out by buyer insolvencies. The modern platform-escrow experiments (escrow.com 1999, Alibaba Trade Assurance 2015, Faire 2017) are attempts to rebuild these rails with software instead of banks and courts.

## 2. Mechanics

### Thread A — the five surviving business models

**1. Transactional network on existing relationships (iTradeNetwork).** Buyers (retailers, foodservice) mandate their existing suppliers onto the network; the platform carries PO → confirmation → ASN → invoice flows plus spec/quality data.

- Born 1999 as TradingProduce.com, a quasi-consortium whose conflicted industry owners [Accel-KKR bought out in a 2007 recap](https://www.accel-kkr.com/case-study/itradenetwork/) — under which it ran >40% revenue CAGR at **>50% EBITDA margins**.
- Connected [over 6,200 customers transacting over $250B annually](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/roper-industries-to-acquire-itradenetwork-inc-99222204.html) by 2010; today claims [~8,000 network companies and ~$50B in invoices processed annually](https://www.itradenetwork.com/about-us) as a Roper Technologies unit.
- It is EDI-with-a-portal for perishables: no matching, no settlement, no title transfer — subscription/network fees, not a take rate. Maps to DTP's contract + traceability layers, with nothing at intent/match/escrow.

**2. Compliance network (ReposiTrak, Trustwell).** Hub-and-spoke: retailers/wholesalers ("hubs") require their suppliers ("spokes") to join and pay.

- ReposiTrak (NYSE: TRAK, née Park City Group) runs supplier compliance-document exchange and now the ReposiTrak Traceability Network — [4,000+ suppliers](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/9812-repositrak-traceability-network-grows-to-4-000-suppliers-furthering-fsma-204-compliance) exchanging FSMA 204 Key Data Elements with retailers, low-hundreds-of-dollars recurring fees per connection, ~99% recurring revenue, no new hardware/software.
- FY2025: [revenue $22.6M (+11%), net income $7.0M (+17%)](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/repositrak-full-year-revenue-increases-11-226-million-net-income-increases-17-70) — tiny but durable and debt-free.
- Instructive wrinkle: when FDA [extended the FSMA 204 deadline to July 20, 2028](https://produceprocessing.net/article/fsma-204-traceability-clock-still-ticking/), ReposiTrak's growth went flat — compliance demand is deadline-elastic. It also reports supplier-submitted traceability data is 50–70% erroneous on first pass.
- Trustwell is the 2022 [merger of FoodLogiQ (traceability SaaS) and ESHA Research (labeling/nutrition)](https://www.trustwell.com/news-and-press/esha-research-and-foodlogiq-merge-to-form-trustwell/) under PE owner Riverside, recapitalized by [TPG's Rise Funds in Jan 2026](https://www.retaildive.com/press-release/20260106-trustwell-announces-strategic-investment-from-tpgs-rise-funds) with ~2,200+ food-company customers.
- Compliance is bought because it is mandatory; trade flows attach to it afterward.

**3. Financing-first (ProducePay, Silo, FBN Finance).**

- ProducePay advances working capital against produce in risk-staged tranches — [~10% of crop value pre-season, ~50% at pick-and-pack, ~80% at invoice on delivery confirmation, historically earning ~2% commission on financed crops](https://idbinvest.org/en/fresh-idea-financing-produce-farmers) — plus "Predictable Commerce" programs where [retailers commit to fixed pricing and volume before the season](https://producepay.com/predictable-commerce-program/) in exchange for vetted supply.
- ProducePay scale: ~$104M equity plus large debt facilities including a [$500M minimum forward-flow commitment from MAVRK Capital](https://perishablenews.com/produce/producepay-partners-with-mavrk-capital-to-make-global-fresh-produce-commerce-more-predictable/); [over $10B in trade facilitated and $3B+ financed to farmers](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/05/producepay-raises-38m-to-tackle-produce-supply-chain-waste/); Series D led by Syngenta, Feb 2024.
- Silo (founded 2018) began as a produce marketplace and became [ERP + payments + financing](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/silo) (Instant Pay, Cash Advance) with a [$100M Jefferies facility](https://usesilo.com/blog/silo-enters-into-financing-with-jefferies-to-help-more-produce-suppliers-and-distributors-unlock-the-cash-flow-needed-to-thrive) and a $32M Series C from Koch plus a $100M First Citizens credit facility (2023).
- In Oct 2024 Silo took a control-style investment from PE firm Banneker, and in Oct 2025 [acquired Pet Tiger/Orange Enterprises](https://www.bannekerpartners.com/announcement/pet-tiger-joins-silo-to-build-the-leading-produce-erp-and-labor-management-platform/) to consolidate produce ERP — i.e., it now competes with the legacy incumbent Produce Pro Software (b. 1990, [acquired by ERP consolidator Aptean in 2022](https://www.aptean.com/en-US/insights/press-release/aptean-extends-its-food-and-beverage-erp-presence-in-the-food-distribution-market-through-the-acquisition-of-produce-pro-software)), not with distributors.
- Notably, ProducePay litigated and won the assignability of PACA trust rights to a factor ([Produce Pay, Inc. v. FVF Distributors](https://calawyers.org/business-law/produce-sellers-collection-rights-and-remedies-under-paca-including-right-to-sue-account-debtor-in-tort-can-be-assigned-to-commercial-factor-produce-pay-inc-v-fvf-distributors-inc/)) — fintechs are building *on top of* the PACA rail, not around it.

**4. Ordering layer on existing distributor relationships (Pepper, Choco, REKKI, Cut+Dry, post-pivot Notch and Cheetah).** These digitize the restaurant↔distributor order flow *without touching the relationship, pricing, credit, or logistics*.

- Pepper sells white-label SaaS e-commerce to [200+ independent distributors, 20x revenue growth since 2021, $30M Series B led by ICONIQ](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240513485816/en/Pepper-Secures-30-Million-in-Series-B-Funding-to-Empower-Independent-Foodservice-Distributors), with a $50M Series C reported in Feb 2026 — funding *accelerating* while the disintermediators died.
- Choco (Berlin, unicorn in 2022, ~$280M raised) pivoted from a restaurant ordering app to [AI order-intake for distributors](https://choco.com/us/stories/suppliers/from-restaurant-app-to-ai-partner-for-distributors-the-evolution-of-choco) ("OrderAgent" converts emails/voicemails to ERP entries at [up to 97% accuracy](https://www.foodlogistics.com/software-technology/ai-ar/news/22879816/choco-ai-solution-to-streamline-order-management-in-foodservice)), claiming 21,000+ distributors and 100,000 buyers, ~$50M ARR.
- [REKKI](https://rekki.com/restaurants) (London, ~$60M raised) does chef ordering + supplier AI; orders to non-member suppliers auto-convert to email — one-sided onboarding by design. [Cut+Dry](https://cutanddry.com/) (founded by the ex-Sysco Labs team) sells e-commerce + AR automation to distributors.
- None of them match counterparties. None of them hold money. That is precisely why distributors let them in the door.

**5. The rollup (GrubMarket) — owning the middleman instead of replacing him.**

- GrubMarket reached [$2B+ revenue in 2024, on track for $2.4B in 2025, $3.5B valuation](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/03/18/us-food-tech-leader-grubmarket-hits-3point5-billion-value-in-new-funding.html), then [$4.5B pre-money at its Feb 2026 Series H](https://www.pymnts.com/supply-chain/2026/grubmarket-hits-4-5-billion-valuation-as-ai-optimizes-food-supply-chain/) and a [confidential IPO filing in July 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/ipo/2026/4-5b-food-tech-firm-grubmarket-submits-confidential-ipo-filing/).
- How: *acquiring 60+ physical distributors* (Delta Fresh, Coast Citrus) and layering its own software/AI on them, plus buying trade-platform [Procurant ($5.5B annual GMV)](https://www.pymnts.com/supply-chain/2026/grubmarket-hits-4-5-billion-valuation-as-ai-optimizes-food-supply-chain/). It is not a marketplace; it is a tech-enabled Sysco built by M&A. The largest "food tech" success in the category is structurally a distributor.
- Caveat on all its self-reported numbers: in Jan 2025 the [SEC found GrubMarket had overstated historical revenue by ~$550M to Series D investors and fined it $8M](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-18) (settled, no admission).

### Thread B — the settlement rails

**PACA trust (the statutory escrow produce sellers already have).**

- Every US buyer of >2,000 lbs/day of fresh/frozen produce must hold a PACA license (~[14,500 licensees](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2007-11-01/pdf/E7-21477.pdf)). Coverage: perishable commodities "in their original state" only — fresh corn yes, canned corn no.
- On delivery, the buyer's produce inventory, products derived from it, *and receivables/proceeds from resale* become a [floating statutory trust](https://www.meuerslawfirm.com/produce-law-academy/paca-trust/paca-trust-101/) for the benefit of unpaid sellers, with [priority over all other creditors including perfected secured lenders, and survival through bankruptcy](https://www.reinhartlaw.com/news-insights/pacas-priority-a-potential-problem-for-secured-lenders) — trust assets are not even property of the bankruptcy estate. Company principals can be held *personally* liable for trust dissipation.
- Conditions: default payment terms are 10 days; parties may agree in writing to at most 30 days — [terms beyond 30 days forfeit trust protection entirely](https://agrisk.umd.edu/post/what-do-produce-suppliers-give-up-when-they-waive-their-paca-trust-rights) — and sellers must preserve rights via statutory invoice language or written notice within 30 days of default.
- It is a *floating* trust with no segregation requirement: protection exists only to the extent assets remain when the seller finally moves. There is no fund; there is a priority claim on whatever is left.
- Enforcement: USDA reparation complaints must be filed [within 9 months, and formal cases average roughly two years to decision](https://mccarronlaw.com/paca/disputes/paca-reparation-cases/); [~3,350 claims involving $63M+ resolved over a recent three-year span](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9212928/usda-paca-complaints-and-reparations-update/). The real teeth: unpaid reparation awards trigger license revocation plus an [employment bar on "responsibly connected" individuals](https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/paca/employment-bonds) — PACA ends careers, not just firms.
- This is why DTP's produce settlement window is ≤30 days: it is a statutory cliff, not a stylistic choice.

**Letters of credit — the 400-year-old escrow and why nobody uses it domestically.**

- Mechanics: buyer's bank promises payment against conforming documents (invoice, bill of lading, inspection cert) under UCP 600 — payment conditional on *evidence*, independent of the goods themselves.
- Cost: typically 0.75–1.5% all-in for the buyer (issuance + confirmation + examination + amendment fees), up to ~3% for risky corridors.
- Share: LCs cover only [~12.5% of world trade ($2.3T), documentary collections another 1.7%](https://www.gtreview.com/news/global/banks-signal-the-death-of-traditional-trade-finance/), and the share keeps falling as open account (~80–90% of trade) dominates.
- The killer defect: [60–75% of document presentations are refused on first presentation for discrepancies](https://www.tradefinance.training/blog/articles/discrepancy-rates-under-ucp-600/) — human document checking made conditional payment so expensive and slow that traders preferred naked credit risk.
- In domestic US trade LCs are essentially absent: [large US companies have historically refused to open LCs](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2007/10/22/open-account-versus-letters-of-credit-for-latin-american-purchasing/), because a shared legal system, credit bureaus (Blue Book in produce), PACA, and trade-credit insurance (~0.1–0.5% of insured sales — an order of magnitude cheaper, covering the whole book) are better substitutes.
- Lesson: conditional-on-evidence settlement is a *correct idea that failed on verification cost* — exactly the cost DTP's delivery attestation + smart-contract release is supposed to collapse.

**Factoring & supply-chain finance.**

- Produce factoring runs at roughly [2.8–3% fees](https://www.bankersfactoring.com/agriculture-paca-factoring/) per invoice cycle (1–5% across agriculture generally), with PACA trust rights assignable to the factor.
- PACA also cuts the other way: under the [*S&H Packing* "true sale" test, a factor whose deal is in substance secured lending must disgorge collections to PACA beneficiaries even without knowledge of the trust](https://nationalaglawcenter.org/court-rules-true-sale-test-applies-assets-paca-trust/) — the statutory rail disciplines the financing rail.
- Early-pay discounting (2/10 net 30) prices working capital at ~36% APR; dynamic-discounting platforms (C2FO, Taulia) run the same trade as an auction against buyer-stretched base terms.
- Reverse-factoring programs that stretch base terms past 30 days [quietly strip produce sellers of PACA trust rights](https://www.stoel.com/insights/publications/paca-vs-pandemic-extending-payment-period-before-b) (cf. the industry backlash to [Kroger's attempted 90-day terms](https://progressivegrocer.com/krogers-90-day-payment-policy-produce-industry-reacts)), and rating agencies flag reverse factoring as "hidden debt" that evaporates exactly when the buyer is distressed (Carillion, Greensill).
- Context: US average DSO is [~47 days](https://www.clearlypayments.com/blog/statistics-on-b2b-payments-in-2026-net30-net60-and-digital-adoption/); B2B checks fell from [81% of payments in 2004 to ~26% in 2025](https://www.nacha.org/news/over-21-years-massive-drop-b2b-check-payments-study-finds) while B2B ACH moved [~$58T in 2024](https://www.tryjeeves.com/blog/b2b-payments-statistics). Trade credit remains the dominant external finance for SMBs, and the embedded-terms fintechs generalizing Faire's model are where value accrues (Melio sold to Xero for $2.5B in 2025; Balance powers net terms for Alibaba.com's US buyers).
- The seller's real pain is the *wait*, not usually the loss — which is why every surviving food-trade fintech (ProducePay, Silo Instant Pay, Notch's AR manager) is a factoring business wearing software clothes, and why DTP's LP-pool advance is the load-bearing seller incentive.

**Alibaba Trade Assurance — platform escrow at scale.** Launched ~2015: buyer pays into the platform; funds are held until shipment/spec criteria are met; disputes are mediated with refund guarantees; free to buyers.

- Scale: [160M+ orders, 37M buyers, 200K suppliers covered](https://smartbuy.alibaba.com/buyingguides/trade-assurance); [in its first full year it extended >$3B of coverage across 1.2M+ orders, and 15% of buyers using it were entirely new to the platform](https://usa.chinadaily.com.cn/epaper/2016-04/14/content_24539985.htm) — escrow demonstrably unlocked demand, not just protected it.
- Escrow-style payment is now used in [over 68% of cross-border B2B orders on Alibaba, up from 45% in 2020](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/apparel-accessories/platform-escrow-payment-guide-alibaba-b2b); for first-time high-value orders it is often [the only payment method buyers will accept](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/other-apparel/escrow-service-payment-secure-transactions-alibaba-b2b).
- Design details that matter for DTP: each supplier carries a public, data-driven [coverage limit that functions as a merchandisable trust score](https://guidedimports.com/blog/alibaba-trade-assurance-overview/) — suppliers compete to raise it via on-time shipment and low dispute rates.
- Coverage extends only to failure modes written into the on-platform order contract, and dispute evidence is admissible only from platform channels (WeChat/email threads don't count) — the contract schema *is* the protection boundary.
- [Third-party inspection (SGS/Bureau Veritas, ~$300) is the decisive evidence in quality disputes](https://www.chinaispp.com/what-is-alibaba-trade-assurance-and-how-does-it-protect-buyers/): escrow works only where specs are objective and verification can be bought.
- This is the strongest empirical evidence that escrow-conditioned settlement *can* be the adoption driver for a B2B marketplace — in a context (cross-border, no shared legal system, unknown counterparties) where trust substitutes are absent.

**Faire — risk absorption as the wedge.** Faire's growth hack was not escrow but the opposite: it *extends* net-60 terms to retailers, [pays brands immediately, and eats the retailer credit risk itself](https://research.contrary.com/company/faire), plus free returns on first orders.

- The wedge worked instantly: within three months of introducing net-60 in late 2017, [monthly GMV went from $100K to $1M](https://research.contrary.com/company/faire).
- The risk was real: in early 2018 retailers over-returned and defaulted en masse; Faire survived only by building real underwriting (credit limits, behavioral scoring, linked bank accounts), cutting return rates 75% in six months.
- Take rate: historically 25% on first orders/15% repeat; [since May 2023, 15% commission + a flat $10 new-customer fee, and 0% (payment processing only) on "Faire Direct" relationships the brand brings itself](https://blog.faire.com/selling/north-america-pricing-update).
- Trajectory: ~[$3B GMV and ~$500M revenue run-rate in 2025, growth accelerating eight straight quarters](https://sacra.com/c/faire/); valuation [$12.6B (2022) → $5.2B (Dec 2025 tender)](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/12/05/b2b-marketplace-faire-reaches-5-2-billion-valuation/).
- Verdict: Faire proves the trust/terms problem is real and worth ~15% of GMV to solve — but it solved it as a balance-sheet underwriter (an insurance business wrapped in a marketplace), not a neutral escrow agent, and it nearly died of its own credit book before underwriting caught up.

**escrow.com & Incoterms.** escrow.com (b. 1999) charges [0.89–3.25%](https://www.escrow.com/support/faqs/how-much-does-it-cost-to-use-escrowcom-services) and had secured [~$7.75B cumulative by April 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/escrow-com-partners-brokerbin-secure-123000866.html) — twenty-five years of volume equal to about three weeks of Sysco revenue; [Freelancer.com bought the whole company in 2015 for $7.5M](https://techcrunch.com/2015/04/26/freelancer-acquires-escrow/). Third-party escrow without an attached marketplace stayed niche because it thrives only on binary-delivery, title-transfer assets (domains, vehicles), inspection/dispute handling doesn't scale for goods where conformity is a matter of degree, and both sides pay friction for protection only one side wants. Incoterms, by contrast, achieved [use in 140+ countries in 31 languages](https://www.freightamigo.com/en/blog/logistics/the-history-of-incoterms-from-1936-to-2020-and-beyond/) with *zero* infrastructure: eleven three-letter codes that pin where cost and risk transfer (note the domestic trap: US contracts default to UCC "F.O.B." terms, which differ from Incoterms FOB, unless Incoterms are expressly incorporated). The cheapest thing to standardize is semantics, and it's also the stickiest.

**The rails compared:**

| Rail | Protection model | Cost to trade | Speed to seller | Works for strangers? | Verdict for DTP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open account + relationship | reputation, repeat business | ~0 | 10–47 days | no | the incumbent to beat |
| PACA trust | statutory super-priority, *ex post* | ~0 (compliance formalities) | months–2 yrs if contested | yes (any licensee) | covers the tail, not the wait |
| Letter of credit | bank pays vs documents, *ex ante* | 0.75–3% | days after conforming docs | yes | right idea, dead on verification cost |
| Factoring | sell the receivable | 1–5%/cycle | days | n/a (post-trade) | prices the wait DTP must beat |
| Trade credit insurance | indemnity on the book | 0.1–0.5% of sales | n/a (loss only) | partially | quiet killer of LCs/escrow domestically |
| Platform escrow (Trade Assurance) | funds held vs attestation | free to buyer | on release | **yes — its whole point** | DTP's closest ancestor |
| Balance-sheet terms (Faire) | platform eats credit risk | ~15% take | immediate | yes | proves demand; DTP decentralizes the balance sheet |
| Third-party escrow (escrow.com) | licensed agent holds funds | 0.9–3.25% | on release + inspection | yes | cautionary: escrow without a market is a niche |

### The pattern, extracted

The adoption curve across twenty-plus years: **spot marketplaces peak at fundraise and decay to pivot; workflow/compliance/financing layers compound quietly.** Not a single neutral food/ag spot marketplace on this list reached durable scale as a marketplace. Every current success either (a) rides a buyer or regulatory mandate, (b) advances money, (c) sells software to the incumbent middleman, or (d) *is* the middleman.

Why, mechanically:

1. **B2B food buying is a repeat-relationship game, not a search game.** A restaurant orders from the same 5–10 distributors weekly on custom-negotiated price lists; a farmer sells to elevators who pay in 72 hours and extend credit. Price discovery among strangers solves a problem these buyers mostly don't have, while destroying what they do value: trust, terms, delivery reliability, recourse.
2. **Spot marketplaces inherit the physical problem.** Indigo (missing loads, months-late payments) and Cheetah (own trucks, ~$140M burned) died on logistics and working capital — the exact functions Cargill/ADM/Sysco are ruthlessly good at. Ordering-layer companies carry zero inventory risk; their COGS is software.
3. **The distributor is the customer, not the enemy.** Pepper, Cut+Dry, and post-pivot Choco sell to distributors; REKKI monetizes the supplier side. This aligns the platform with the party that has transaction density and willingness to pay.
4. **Every "marketplace" that lived did so by becoming workflow or fintech for existing trades:** ChefHero→Notch (ordering→AP/AR), FarmLead→Combyne (spot→contract ledger, exit to Bayer), Indigo Marketplace→Market+ (exchange→SaaS *for elevators*), Silo (marketplace→ERP+payments), Forager→Local Line (open matching→anchor-buyer programs).
5. **Vanity liquidity never converts.** Indigo's $6B of "listed" grain produced no durable trade flow, because listings are free and switching counterparties is expensive. The durable wedge is the order/invoice/payment rail of trades that were already going to happen.

## 4. Incentive Autopsy

- **Who paid?** In the survivor models, one of three parties with a burning reason:
  - the party with the compliance obligation (suppliers pay ReposiTrak because retailers/FDA make them);
  - the party desperate for cash (growers pay ProducePay ~2%+financing spread happily, because the alternative is a 47-day DSO or a 3% factor);
  - the incumbent distributor buying software to defend its book (Pepper/Choco/Cut+Dry).
  - In the corpse models: VCs paid, because neither side of a spot trade would.
- **Who captured the value?**
  - Faire captured 15–25% of GMV for absorbing risk; GrubMarket captures distributor margin itself; iTrade captured a toll on order flow that buyers mandated.
  - Farmers captured almost nothing from Indigo/FarmLead — which is *why* those failed: the promised value (better basis, price discovery) was real but too thin and too infrequent to change grain-selling habits anchored in local elevator relationships that settle in 72 hours and extend credit.
- **Who bore integration cost?** Always the supplier side (onboarding, catalog, EDI/ERP hookup). That is why the only two working onboarding strategies are:
  - mandates (iTrade, ReposiTrak — the buyer forces the spokes on), or
  - zero-integration wedges (Choco parsing the *existing* email/voicemail order with no ERP change; REKKI auto-converting orders to email for non-member suppliers).
- **The middleman problem.** The claimed villain — the distributor — never actually got disintermediated in a single case in this dossier.
  - Cheetah tried to *be* the distributor with an app and ended up selling the trucks.
  - ChefHero tried to route around distributors and now sells them AR/AP software.
  - The one company that "beat" distributors, GrubMarket, did it by buying them.
  - Meanwhile new middlemen did appear: the platforms themselves (Faire's 15–25% is a fatter take than most distributors' *net* margin; Alibaba holds the float and the data; the compliance networks toll the mandate).

## 5. What's Right

- **Escrow-conditioned settlement drives adoption when trust substitutes are absent.** Trade Assurance's [68% escrow penetration on cross-border orders](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/apparel-accessories/platform-escrow-payment-guide-alibaba-b2b) is the cleanest large-n validation of DTP's core mechanic that exists.
- **Payment-terms arbitrage is the proven wedge in wholesale.** Faire (net-60 to buyer, instant to seller) and ProducePay/Silo (advance to seller) both found that *who waits for money* is the sharpest pain in the entire trade — sharper than price discovery, matching, or logistics.
- **PACA proves conditional, super-priority seller protection is compatible with a functioning market** — and that a *statutory* settlement deadline (30 days) can shape industry behavior for 40 years.
- **Compliance mandates are legitimate adoption rails.** ReposiTrak built a profitable network on FSMA 204 with a flat-fee, no-new-software onboarding pitch. DTP's FSMA 204 traceability layer is the part of its stack with a guaranteed buyer.
- **Standardize semantics first.** Incoterms conquered world trade as a vocabulary. A protocol's cheapest, most durable artifact is its shared definitions (what "delivered," "accepted," "cured" mean) — DTP's attestation schema is its Incoterms.
- **The ordering-layer companies' zero-displacement insight:** meet the trade where it already happens (email, voicemail, text), digitize it in place, and only then add services. Choco's order-parsing is agent-native commerce bootstrapped on legacy message formats — structurally identical to what DTP's agents must do to onboard non-DTP counterparties.

## 6. What's Wrong / Failure Modes

- **Neutral spot marketplaces died of liquidity anemia — and of the physical layer.** Grain: Indigo Marketplace launched with a [$250M Series E](https://agfundernews.com/breaking-indigo-raises-250m-series-e-adding-grain-marketplace-to-farm-services-platform) and boasted $6B of grain "listed" — listings, not trades. The operational reality per [Bloomberg's post-mortem](https://governorswindenergycoalition.org/how-a-billion-dollar-farm-tech-startup-stumbled-then-revamped/): contracted trucks showed up for farm pickups in the middle of the night, grain loads went missing, and farmers waited *months* for payment when the local elevator pays in 72 hours — some got paid only after threatening regulators; a co-op took Indigo to arbitration. Within three years Indigo [removed itself from the transaction flow](https://www.indigoag.com/pages/news/introducing-marketplus-by-indigo), reselling the tech as Market+ *to the grain elevators it had tried to displace*, and its valuation fell [~95% from ~$4B to ~$200M](https://www.energyconnects.com/news/renewables/2024/june/farm-startups-bent-on-shaking-up-cargill-adm-are-stumbling/) before the company pivoted to carbon credits. FarmLead concluded its marketplace ["was not truly solving major problems"](https://welcome.combyne.ag/about/) and sunset it. Foodservice: ChefHero and Cheetah's models collapsed. The recurring anatomy: commodity trades have thin margins, existing counterparties, and relationship-embedded services (credit, agronomy advice, freight, recourse, 72-hour settlement); a matching engine adds a few basis points of price improvement against all of that — while inheriting logistics and settlement obligations it executes worse than the incumbents.
- **"Marketplace" is a costume.** Nearly every survivor that still says marketplace is actually a lender (ProducePay), an ERP (Silo), or a distributor (GrubMarket). Investors funded matching; revenue came from balance sheet and workflow.
- **Full-stack disintermediation burns capital at distributor economics.** Cheetah raised $100M+ to run app-based distribution and exited by selling the physical business — you cannot out-margin an incumbent whose whole moat *is* operating at 3% margin.
- **Escrow without a marketplace attached stays niche** (escrow.com: ~$7.75B cumulative over 25 years — Sysco does that in about a month). Protection that adds friction to *both* sides gets bought only for rare, high-variance trades.
- **LCs show conditional settlement fails when verification is manual:** 60–75% first-presentation discrepancy rates turned a sound mechanism into a fee machine that open account displaced.
- **PACA's limits (the honest read for DTP):** protection is remedial, not preventative — the seller still must chase a defaulting buyer, trust assets may already be dissipated, rights are forfeited by sloppy paperwork or by [agreeing to terms over 30 days](https://agrisk.umd.edu/post/what-do-produce-suppliers-give-up-when-they-waive-their-paca-trust-rights), and enforcement means lawyers. But it *does* cover the catastrophic tail, which weakens "you might not get paid" as an escrow sales pitch for domestic produce.

## 7. Kill Conditions

**What killed the dead:**

- No urgent, frequent pain — grain sells a few times a year into known elevators; price discovery is an occasional nicety, not a daily workflow.
- The value-add (price discovery) was worth less than the bundled services it displaced (credit, 72-hour settlement, freight, agronomy, recourse).
- Supply-side onboarding cost with no mandate — nobody joins a two-sided network for a maybe-trade.
- Full-stack models hit distributor cost structure: Cheetah burned ~$140M learning that trucks and warehouses price at 3% operating margins regardless of the app on top.
- Execution on the physical/settlement layer they inherited: Indigo's missing grain loads and months-late payments did more damage than any pricing flaw.
- COVID killed foodservice demand for the marginal players (ChefHero's "extinction-level event").

**What keeps the survivors alive:**

- iTrade — switching costs plus buyer mandates on order flow.
- ReposiTrak/Trustwell — federal regulation with a 2028 deadline (and deadline-elastic growth to match).
- ProducePay/Silo — interest margin, the oldest business in agriculture, now PE/forward-flow funded.
- Pepper/Choco/REKKI/Cut+Dry — they make the incumbent stronger, so the incumbent pays.
- GrubMarket — it owns the assets.
- Faire — brand-side acquisition loop plus underwriting data flywheel (though the $12.6B→$5.2B markdown shows risk absorption at a ~15% take is capital-market-fragile, not invincible).
- PACA/Incoterms — statutory/ICC lock-in and zero marginal cost.

**What would kill DTP by the same logic:**

- Launching as a neutral matching venue for counterparties who already know each other.
- Underpricing the bundled distributor services it displaces, then discovering the trade needed them.
- Making escrow feel like friction to the buyer — who in domestic produce currently pays *after* delivery with PACA behind them. Escrow asks the buyer to pay *earlier* to protect a seller who already holds a super-priority trust; the buyer must get something back (better price, access, or terms via the LP pool) or will not come.
- Inheriting the physical/settlement layer without executing it: one Indigo-grade season of late releases or botched attestations ends protocol credibility with farmers permanently.

## 8. Null Hypothesis Check

**Would the trades happen fine without any of this?** Mostly yes — and quantifying the "20–30% middleman extraction" claim honestly is sobering:

- The [USDA food dollar](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-dollar/summary-findings): farm share 15.9¢, marketing share 84.1¢ (2023). But that 84¢ includes processing, retail labor, foodservice preparation, transport — it is *not* a coordination tax. The trade layers specifically: [wholesale trade takes 11.4¢ and retail trade 14.7¢ of the food dollar](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/110567) — the *entire* wholesale layer, services included, is ~11 cents.
- Broadline distribution, the actual "middleman" layer DTP targets: Sysco FY2025 [gross margin 18.4% on $81.4B sales, operating income $3.1B (~3.8%)](https://investors.sysco.com/~/media/Files/S/Sysco-IR/documents/quarterly-results/syy-2025-q4-press-release.pdf); US Foods [gross 17.4%, adjusted EBITDA margin 4.9%](https://ir.usfoods.com/newsroom/news/news-details/2026/US-Foods-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Earnings/default.aspx). Produce wholesalers historically mark up [~18% or less to supermarkets](https://www.fao.org/4/w3240e/W3240E12.htm); pure brokers take low single digits.
- So of an ~18-point gross margin, roughly 13–15 points buy real bundled services — trucks, cold chain, warehouses, sales reps, credit (the distributor *is* the bank for 21–30 days), aggregation (one PO for 200 SKUs), demand smoothing, and recourse (shorted/rejected product gets credited without litigation). The residual economic profit is low single digits. **The pure coordination tax is closer to 3–8% than 20–30%** — larger in fragmented specialty/produce channels with multiple handling steps (grower → shipper 8–10% commission → wholesaler → distributor can stack to 25–35% farm-to-kitchen spread), but each step still carries physical work.
- On the settlement side, the null holds even harder for domestic produce: open account + PACA + Blue Book ratings + factoring already move the goods. Checks-to-ACH migration happened without any protocol. What the status quo genuinely does *not* provide: fast seller liquidity (47-day DSO; factoring costs ~3%/cycle), trust between *strangers* (which is why direct grower↔buyer trade that skips the stack doesn't happen more), machine-readable trade state, and cheap conditional settlement. That — not "middlemen are parasites" — is the defensible gap.

**Implication:** DTP's honest pitch is not "recover the 20–30% the middlemen steal." It is:

1. Enable the direct trades that today don't happen at all because strangers can't trust each other (the Trade Assurance case — escrow as market-creating, not margin-recovering).
2. Compress the working-capital cost of the trades that do happen (the Faire/ProducePay case — beat the 3%/cycle factor and the 47-day DSO).
3. Let the parties who genuinely don't need the distributor bundle (full-pallet, repeat, spec-stable lanes) drop 5–15 points of stacked margin — while conceding the rest of the volume to bundled distribution, possibly forever.

## 9. Lessons for DTP

**Steal:**
1. **Lead with money, not matching.** Every food/ag survivor monetizes financing or workflow. DTP's LP-pool seller advance is not a nice-to-have; it is the product. Sellers should experience DTP as "get paid in days instead of 47, cheaper than a 3% factor," with escrow as the machinery that makes the advance safe to underwrite.
2. **Price escrow against PACA, explicitly.** For PACA-covered produce, escrow's marginal value over the statutory trust is *certainty and speed* (funds provably exist and release on attestation, no reparation docket, no trust-preservation paperwork, no dissipated-asset risk). For non-PACA goods (>30-day terms, exempt commodities, processed foods), escrow is the *only* protection — target those first; the value gap is bigger.
3. **Copy Trade Assurance's asymmetry: free to buyers.** Alibaba charges buyers nothing for assurance and recovers value through supplier-side services and flow. A buyer asked to prepay into escrow must be compensated — via price, LP-pool-funded terms (buyer settles at 30/60 days while seller was paid at attestation — DTP can replicate Faire's net-60 wedge without Faire's balance sheet), or access to supply.
4. **Attestation is the whole ballgame.** The LC's 60–75% discrepancy rate and escrow.com's inspection-dispute friction both died on verification cost. DTP's delivery-attestation design (who signs, what sensor/inspection evidence suffices, how produce-quality disputes resolve within the 24–72h acceptance window PACA norms allow) deserves more design effort than matching, contracts, and tokenomics combined. USDA inspection (the existing neutral arbiter for produce condition disputes) should be a first-class oracle.
5. **Ride the FSMA 204 mandate** (deadline 2028). Compliance is the one layer with a statutorily guaranteed buyer; ReposiTrak proves flat-fee + zero-new-software onboarding works. Traceability data as a *byproduct* of settling on DTP is a cleaner pitch than traceability as homework.
6. **Standardize semantics like Incoterms.** DTP's schemas for intent, spec, acceptance, and cure should be adoptable by parties who use *none* of DTP's rails — vocabulary spreads further than infrastructure and pulls infrastructure behind it.
7. **Onboard like Choco, not like an exchange.** Agents that parse the existing order flow (email, SMS, PDFs) and mirror it into protocol state let one side adopt DTP unilaterally. Two-sided simultaneous adoption is the marketplace death march.

**Avoid:**
1. **Don't be a neutral spot marketplace for existing relationships** — the single most reliably fatal position in this dossier (Indigo, FarmLead, ChefHero, Cheetah v1, and ~1,500 dot-com ancestors). DTP's matching layer should activate where relationships *don't* exist (new lanes, surplus, import/export-style stranger trades) and stay out of the way where they do (0% take on brought-your-own-counterparty, as Faire concedes).
2. **Don't claim the 20–30%.** The recoverable coordination tax on bundled-service trades is single digits; overclaiming invites the correct rebuttal from every distributor CFO with a 3.8% operating margin.
3. **Don't touch the physical layer.** Cheetah and GrubMarket mark the two poles: touching trucks means becoming a distributor (fine, but that's a different company). DTP must stay protocol — cold chain, aggregation, and demand smoothing are services counterparties procure, attested on DTP, not services DTP performs.
4. **Don't let escrow read as distrust-tax.** Domestic buyers currently pay ~30 days after delivery with goods in hand. A protocol that demands day-0 locked funds with no offsetting benefit selects for exactly the desperate counterparties you don't want (adverse selection — the escrow.com trap).
5. **Don't assume statutory rails are competitors.** PACA trust rights are assignable (ProducePay v. FVF); DTP's LP pool advancing against attestation can *hold* PACA trust and reparation rights as recourse collateral — the 1930 rail becomes DTP's credit backstop rather than its rival.
