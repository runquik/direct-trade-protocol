# Dossier Template

Every dossier in this folder follows this structure so approaches can be compared layer-by-layer. The methodology is adapted from idea-autopsy practice (genome-style): dissect the mechanism, correct for survivorship bias, name kill conditions, and test the null hypothesis before drawing lessons.

---

# <Approach / Protocol Family Name>

**Era:** <years active>
**Status:** <dominant | active | niche | zombie | dead>
**One-line:** <what it is in one sentence>

## 1. Origin Story

Who created it, when, and what specific pain drove it. What did trade coordination look like *before* it existed?

## 2. Mechanics

How it actually works: message formats, state machines, identity model, settlement model (if any), governance. Concrete enough that a protocol designer could compare it to DTP's primitives (intent → match → contract → escrow → attestation → settlement → traceability).

## 3. Adoption Trajectory

How it spread (or didn't). Who adopted first, what the adoption curve looked like, where it plateaued. Include real numbers where findable.

## 4. Incentive Autopsy

- Who paid for it?
- Who captured the value?
- Who bore the integration cost?
- Was there a middleman problem it claimed to solve — and did a new middleman appear (VANs, networks, consortia operators)?

## 5. What's Right

What it genuinely got correct — design decisions that survived, ideas worth stealing.

## 6. What's Wrong / Failure Modes

Design flaws, adoption failures, misaligned incentives. For dead projects: the actual cause of death, not the press-release version.

## 7. Kill Conditions

What would have to be true for this approach to die (or what was true when it did die). For survivors: what keeps it alive despite its flaws.

## 8. Null Hypothesis Check

Would the trade have happened fine without it? What did it *actually* add over phone/fax/email/spreadsheet — and was that enough to pay for itself?

## 9. Lessons for DTP

Specific, actionable implications for a platform-agnostic, agent-native, escrow-settled physical goods protocol. Both "steal this" and "avoid this."

## Sources

Markdown links to everything cited.
