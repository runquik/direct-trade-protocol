# `dtp/inventory@2`

Status: **candidate, not registered.** September 23, 2026. Step 3 of the [business-fact profiles build order](../../../docs/foundation/business-fact-profiles-proposal.md). The kind enters `spec/profiles/index.json` at step 4, once a second, independent implementation has passed the fixtures. Until then a host carries it by admitting the exact contract in [`2/profile.json`](2/profile.json) (publisher `dtp`, semantics `inventory-v2`).

## Name and version

Kind `dtp/inventory@2`; profile `dtp.inventory/2`; contract version `2.0.0`. It does not supersede `dtp.inventory/1` in place: the two are different kinds and may be admitted side by side. Minor versions within the major MUST be additive.

## Nature

An **event** kind whose reducer maintains a **state**: one company's ledger for one product. A fact is immutable and is never superseded; corrections are further facts. The ledger is derived and is not itself a record.

## Identity

- The ledger is keyed by the product: a fact's `resource_id` and its `product_id` are the root of the company's `dtp/product@1` record, and a host opens a ledger from that product's head (`inventory.open`) before accepting facts. The product's `base_unit` and `tracking` are fixed for its life, so the ledger copies them.
- Each fact is its own record (`root_id = record_id`, `supersedes = null`). A fact that cites an order or a transformation names that entity's root in `links`; a fact that another fact cites as evidence is named by its exact `record_id`.
- Positions, lots and locations are keys inside the ledger, not entities. A location or lot that no fact has touched does not exist.

## Payload

Schema in the bounded `dtp.schema/1` dialect with `nullable`, in [`2/profile.json`](2/profile.json). In prose:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `product_id` | The ledger's product root. |
| `observation` | `{source_id, sequence}`: the producing source and its own strictly increasing sequence. Two facts with one observation are one observation. |
| `occurred_at` | Physical time, UTC, millisecond precision or coarser. Distinct from the host's acceptance time. |
| `expected_revision` | The ledger revision the writer decided on. A mismatch is a `revision_conflict`; reload and decide again. |
| `moves` | 0 to 16 legs, each `{from, to, quantity, serial_ids, reason, links}`. Applied in order, atomically: one refused leg refuses the fact. |
| `reservations` | 0 to 16 operations `{kind: reserve \| release \| fulfill, reservation_id, position, quantity, party}`, applied after the moves, atomically with them. A fact carries at least one leg or operation. |

A **position** is `{lot_id, location_id, status}` with `status` one of `available`, `quarantine`, `damaged`, `expired`, `in_transit`. A location whose id starts with `~` is virtual and must be one of `~supplier`, `~customer`, `~adjustment`, `~production`; a virtual position holds nothing and checks nothing. For a real position, `lot_id` is required when the product is lot- or serial-tracked and forbidden otherwise.

A **quantity** is `{amount, unit}` with a positive three-place decimal string and a closed unit object: `{system: "ucum", code, packaging_id: null, version: null, base_units_per_pack: null}` where `code` must equal the product's base unit, or `{system: "packaging", code: null, packaging_id, version, base_units_per_pack}` naming a packaging revision the product published. A pack conversion must yield a whole number of thousandths of the base unit; nothing is ever rounded and no unit is ever converted into another.

## Semantics (`inventory-v2`)

1. **Double entry.** Every leg moves `quantity` from `from` to `to`; both cannot be virtual and they cannot be the same position. Receipts come from `~supplier`, shipments go to `~customer`, count corrections go to or from `~adjustment` and require a `reason`, transformation inputs and outputs go to or from `~production` and require `links.transformation_id`. A status change is a leg between two positions that differ only in status.
2. **Stock is never negative and never reserved twice.** A leg from a real position needs `quantity` of *unreserved* stock there: on hand minus the reservations claiming that position.
3. **Reservations** are claims by a party on an `available` real position, keyed by `reservation_id`; one reservation claims one position. `reserve` needs unreserved stock; `release` and `fulfill` need an existing claim of at least the quantity; `fulfill` also removes the stock from the position (it left for the customer). A serialized product is never fulfilled this way: it ships by a leg naming its serials, plus a `release`.
4. **Serials.** For a serial-tracked product every leg names distinct `serial_ids` whose count equals the quantity; a serial is in exactly one position; a leg from a real position moves serials that are there, and a leg from a virtual one introduces serials that exist nowhere in the ledger. A non-serialized product carries `serial_ids: null`.
5. **Observations.** Per source the ledger keeps a high-water mark and the digests of accepted sequences within a window of 64 below it. A sequence at or below the mark that is in the window: same digest is a duplicate (accepted, no effect), a different digest is an `observation_conflict`. A sequence below the window is refused as `stale_observation`: it cannot be told from a duplicate, and a late fact must not become new stock. A sequence above the mark, or inside the window and unseen, is new. Sources therefore number their observations; a source that cannot wraps its rows in one numbered fact each. State grows with the number of sources, not of facts.
6. **Revision.** `expected_revision` must equal the ledger's revision; every accepted non-duplicate fact increments it. Empty positions are dropped.
7. Refusal codes are normative: `invalid`, `wrong_scope`, `observation_conflict`, `stale_observation`, `revision_conflict`, `unit_mismatch`, `tracking_mismatch`, `insufficient_stock`, `insufficient_reservation`, `serial_conflict`.

A host additionally verifies that every packaging unit in a fact is a revision the product published (`unknown_packaging`), keeps the fact index for idempotent retries, and replays every fact on snapshot import, which must reproduce the ledger exactly.

## Relations

- `dtp/product@1` supplies the base unit, tracking and packaging revisions; its immutability rules exist for this ledger.
- `dtp.inventory/1`: a v1 pool is one location, no lot, status `available`. `projectInventoryV1(ledger, location_id)` is the bridge: a v1-shaped view of one location that a bridge module publishes to v1 subscribers. It is derived, marked so, and cannot be turned back into a ledger. Both kinds may be admitted at one host during a transition; no fact is rewritten from one to the other.
- GS1 EPCIS: a leg with `~supplier` or `~customer` corresponds to an ObjectEvent with `bizStep` receiving or shipping; a status to a `disposition`; `~production` legs to a TransformationEvent's inputs and outputs. That mapping is informative and is not a conformance target; aggregation (handling units) is out of scope (P9).

## Fixtures

[`2/fixtures.json`](2/fixtures.json): two fact streams (a lot-tracked product exercising the whole vocabulary with refusals interleaved, and a serialized product) with the outcome of every fact, the exact final ledger, and the v1 projection of one location, plus the contract digest. Regenerate with `node sdk/scripts/build-inventory-profile.ts`; `--check` verifies. Reference implementation: `sdk/src/profiles/inventory2.ts`, portable and dependency-free, reachable as `inventory2` from both preview entries.

## Not in scope

Handling units and aggregation, ownership versus custody, valuation, expiry-driven status changes, unit conversion, multi-company ledgers, and any claim that a position reflects physical reality. A ledger is what a company's producers said happened, checked for consistency.
