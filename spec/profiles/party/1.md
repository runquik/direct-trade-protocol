# `dtp/party@1`

Status: **registered** in [`spec/profiles/index.json`](../index.json) on September 23, 2026, after a second, separately coded implementation (`sdk/tests/interop/party-reader.ts`) passed the fixtures. Step 5 of the [business-fact profiles build order](../../../docs/foundation/business-fact-profiles-proposal.md). A host admits the exact contract in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json) (publisher `dtp`, semantics `party-v1`) with `profile.admit`.

## Name and version

Kind `dtp/party@1`; profile `dtp.party/1`; contract version `1.0.0`. Minor versions within the major MUST be additive.

## Nature

A **state** kind. One record chain is one counterparty *as this company knows it*: a customer, a supplier, a carrier, a site of one of them, or a person the company deals with. The head is the current statement; revisions supersede.

## Identity

- The entity is the record `root_id`, the writer's id at genesis. There is no natural business key: a GLN, a DUNS number or a tax id is an identifier the company records, namespaced by its scheme, not the identity.
- A revision is the record `record_id`. An order that names a party as buyer or seller names the root, and the exact revision when the order is evidence.
- A party is the company's own record about someone else. Linking it to a protocol organization (`protocol_identity`) is a **claim by the writer**, not proof that the party is that organization, and never grants that organization anything.

## Payload

Schema in the bounded `dtp.schema/1` dialect with `nullable`, in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json). In prose, drawn from UBL `Party` and the EDIFACT `NAD` segment for shape:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `kind` | `organization`, `person` or `unit` (a site, branch or department of a parent party). |
| `names` | 1 to 8 `{name, language}`; the first is the primary name. |
| `identifiers` | 0 to 32 `{scheme, value}`, distinct. `gs1.gln` is check-digit validated; `duns` is nine digits; every other scheme (`tax.<jurisdiction>.<kind>`, a registry number, a customer number) is opaque and namespaced by the writer's choice, as EDIFACT qualifies a party id by its agency. |
| `protocol_identity` | `{organization_id}` or null: the claim that this party is that protocol organization. |
| `roles` | 0 to 8 distinct values from `customer`, `supplier`, `carrier`, `financer`, `service_provider`, `manufacturer`, `broker`, `other`. **Descriptive only.** No module may derive any permission from a role. |
| `locations` | 0 to 32 `{location_id, name, address, gln}`, distinct by id. `address` is `{lines[0..4], locality, region, postal_code, country}` with an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country; `gln` is check-digit validated. GLNs belong to locations, as in v0.2. |
| `parent` | Root of the parent party; required for a `unit`, forbidden otherwise. |
| `status` | `active` or `inactive`. |

Member names follow the repository rule.

## Semantics (`party-v1`)

1. The body passes every rule in the payload table (`validateParty`).
2. Across a supersession of one root (`checkPartyContinuity`): `kind` and `parent` never change, and a `protocol_identity` claim, once made, is neither changed nor withdrawn. A party that turns out to be someone else is a new party.
3. A `person` party is personal data. A host MUST accept it only under a policy classified `personnel` (v0.4 §4); a business-classified policy refuses it with `invalid_party`. Contact details are out of this kind and belong with the person under the same policy.
4. A refused write is `invalid_party`; snapshot import re-checks rules 1 to 3.

## Relations

- `dtp/order@1` names a party as buyer or seller by root.
- The v0.2 `core.company` spine is the *company's own* identity; a party is the company's record of *someone else*. `business_types` there and `roles` here are both descriptive, never permissions.
- A `protocol_identity` claim may be checked against the organization's genesis and governance by a module; the profile makes no such check.

## Not in scope

Contact channels, credit terms, ratings, relationship tiers (v0.2 §6.9 keeps those as derived views), deduplication across parties, verification of legal existence, and any claim that a party has consented to anything.
