# `dtp/forecast@1`

Status: **registered** in [`spec/profiles/index.json`](../index.json) on September 23, 2026, after a second, separately coded implementation (`sdk/tests/interop/forecast-reader.ts`) passed the fixtures. Decision P14 of the [business-fact profiles build order](../../../docs/foundation/business-fact-profiles-proposal.md): the "never an order" rule needs a home for the other thing. A host admits the exact contract in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json) (publisher `dtp`, semantics `forecast-v1`) with `profile.admit`.

## Name and version

Kind `dtp/forecast@1`; profile `dtp.forecast/1`; contract version `1.0.0`. Minor versions within the major MUST be additive.

## Nature

A **state** kind. One record chain is one projection: a figure for a subject over a horizon, from a method, as of a moment. Revisions re-forecast the same thing.

**A forecast commits nobody to anything.** It is not an order, not a plan a counterparty has accepted, not a reservation of stock. A workspace that shows a forecast in an orders list, or a module that reserves stock on one, has a bug. This is the domain map's distinction between plan, commitment and actual made into a rule, and the reason `order@1` carries no flag.

## Identity

- The entity is the record `root_id`. A revision is the record `record_id`.
- `subject`, `party_id`, `direction`, `horizon` and `scenario` identify what is being forecast and are immutable; a different one is a new forecast. A chain labelled `base` stays `base`, so a subscriber comparing scenarios never sees one turn into another.

## Payload

Schema in the bounded `dtp.schema/1` dialect with `nullable`, in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json):

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `subject` | `{product_id, description}`: a product root, a description, or both. |
| `party_id` | The party the projection concerns (a customer's demand, a supplier's supply) or null. |
| `direction` | `demand` or `supply`. |
| `horizon` | `{start, end}` dates, end after start. |
| `as_of` | The UTC instant the figure was computed. Never earlier in a revision than in what it revises. |
| `method` | What produced the figure: a model, a rule, a person's judgement. Required, because a figure without its method is not evidence of anything. |
| `quantity` | A nonnegative three-place decimal in a UCUM unit or a packaging revision (the `inventory@2` unit object). |
| `confidence` | 0 to 100 or null. |
| `scenario` | A label for competing projections (`base`, `high`, `promo`) or null. Fixed for the life of the chain. |
| `status` | `draft`, `published`, `withdrawn`; forward only, by the transition table below. `withdrawn` is terminal. |

## Semantics (`forecast-v1`)

1. The body passes every rule in the payload table (`validateForecast`).
2. **Genesis** (`checkForecastGenesis`): a forecast is born `draft` or `published`, never `withdrawn`.
3. **Revisions** (`checkForecastContinuity`): `subject`, `party_id`, `direction`, `horizon` and `scenario` are immutable; `as_of` never goes back; and every revision, one that keeps the status included, is listed in this table (`FORECAST_TRANSITIONS`):

   | From | To |
   |---|---|
   | `draft` | `draft`, `published`, `withdrawn` |
   | `published` | `published`, `withdrawn` |
   | `withdrawn` | nothing: a withdrawn forecast takes no revision |

   A draft may be abandoned, withdrawn without ever being published. A published forecast may be re-forecast and stay published. A correction to a withdrawn forecast is a new forecast. This mirrors `order@1`, whose terminal statuses take no revision (#76).
4. A refused write is `invalid_forecast`; snapshot import re-checks all three rules.

## Relations

- `dtp/order@1` is what a forecast is not. Nothing in this profile references orders, and a subscriber to orders never receives forecasts, since they are distinct kinds.
- `dtp/product@1` and `dtp/party@1` supply the roots a forecast names.
- Demand and production planning in the domain map: forecast scenarios with method and as-of time. Committed plans, capacity calendars and material requirements are later kinds.

## Not in scope

Forecast accuracy, aggregation across scenarios, the models themselves, and any planning that turns a projection into a commitment.
