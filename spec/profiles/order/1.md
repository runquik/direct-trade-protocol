# `dtp/order@1`

Status: **registered** in [`spec/profiles/index.json`](../index.json) on September 23, 2026, after a second, separately coded implementation (`sdk/tests/interop/order-reader.ts`) passed the fixtures. Step 5 of the [business-fact profiles build order](../../../docs/foundation/business-fact-profiles-proposal.md). A host admits the exact contract in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json) (publisher `dtp`, semantics `order-v1`) with `profile.admit`.

## Name and version

Kind `dtp/order@1`; profile `dtp.order/1`; contract version `1.0.0`. Minor versions within the major MUST be additive.

## Nature

A **state** kind. One record chain is one order as one company records it. The head is the current statement; revisions supersede.

**A forecast or a plan is never an order.** An `order@1` record is a binding commercial commitment by the party that placed it. A producer MUST NOT publish an order for projected, planned, simulated or recommended demand or supply, and a subscriber MAY treat every order as a commitment without inspecting a flag. Projections belong to [`dtp/forecast@1`](../forecast/1.md), whose profile says the opposite: nothing in it commits anyone. There is no `commitment` field (decision P10): the kind is the commitment.

## Identity

- The entity is the record `root_id`, the writer's id at genesis. A buyer's PO number or a seller's order number is an external identifier, namespaced by its scheme, not the identity.
- A revision is the record `record_id`. A stock move that fulfils a line cites the order root in its `links`; evidence of what was agreed cites the exact revision.
- One of `buyer` and `seller` is the company itself (`self: true`); the other is a [`dtp/party@1`](../party/1.md) root the company recorded. The company writes every revision of its own order; the roles say whose act a revision records, and cross-company agreement remains the job of a signed `trade.contract`, which a line may cite.

## Payload

Schema in the bounded `dtp.schema/1` dialect with `nullable`, in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json). Drawn from UBL 2.4 `Order` and `OrderResponse`, reduced to what a workspace needs:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `buyer`, `seller` | `{self, party_id, revision}`: exactly one has `self: true` (and no party); the other names a party root and optionally the exact party revision. |
| `placed_at` | UTC millisecond instant at which the order was placed. |
| `currency` | Uppercase code, 3 to 8 letters (ISO 4217 or a monetary asset label, as `invoice-v1` allows). Every line price is in it. |
| `external` | 0 to 16 `{scheme, value}`, distinct: the buyer's PO number, the seller's order number, and the like. |
| `lines` | 1 to 256 `{line_id, product, quantity, price, requested, links}`, distinct by id. `product` names a product root (and optionally its exact revision) or carries a description, or both. `quantity` is a positive three-place decimal in a UCUM unit or a packaging revision (the `inventory@2` unit object). `price` is a six-place money amount in the order currency or null. `requested` is a date window or null. `links.contract_id` cites a v0.2 contract root or null. |
| `status` | `placed`, `acknowledged`, `rejected`, `partially_fulfilled`, `fulfilled`, `cancelled`, `closed`. |
| `terms` | `{payment_net_days: 0..365 or null, incoterm: three letters or null}`. |
| `replaces` | Root of the order this one replaces, or null. Amending an acknowledged order is a new order. |

## Semantics (`order-v1`)

1. The body passes every rule in the payload table (`validateOrder`).
2. **Genesis** is born `placed` (`checkOrderGenesis`).
3. **Revisions** (`checkOrderContinuity`): `buyer`, `seller`, `placed_at`, `currency` and `replaces` never change. The status follows the table below. While `placed`, lines and terms may be edited; from `acknowledged` on they are frozen, and a change is a new order that `replaces` this one, matching the v0.2 rule that commercial terms are immutable once agreed. External identifiers may be added, never removed.
4. A refused write is `invalid_order`; snapshot import re-checks rules 1 to 3.

| From | To | Whose act it records |
|---|---|---|
| `placed` | `acknowledged`, `rejected` | the seller |
| `placed` | `cancelled` | either party |
| `acknowledged` | `partially_fulfilled`, `fulfilled` | the seller, citing stock moves as evidence |
| `acknowledged`, `partially_fulfilled` | `cancelled` | either party |
| `partially_fulfilled` | `partially_fulfilled`, `fulfilled` | the seller |
| `fulfilled` | `closed` | the buyer |
| `rejected`, `cancelled`, `closed` | nothing | terminal |

## Relations

- `dtp/party@1` supplies the counterparty; `dtp/product@1` the product roots and revisions; `dtp/inventory@2` moves cite the order root in `links.order_id`.
- `trade.contract` (v0.2) is the cross-company, counterparty-signed agreement in a store; an order is the company's own working state and MAY cite one. Where both exist the contract is the evidence. UBL's `Order` and `OrderResponse` map onto the buyer's `placed` revision and the seller's `acknowledged` revision of one entity.
- `dtp/forecast@1` is what an order is not.

## Not in scope

Pricing and tax computation, delivery scheduling, invoicing (that is `invoice-v1`), returns and disputes, and any proof that the counterparty agreed. An order record is one company's statement of a commitment; the other side's statement is its own record.
