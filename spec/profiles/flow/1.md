# `dtp/flow@1`

Status: **registered** in [`spec/profiles/index.json`](../index.json) on October 22, 2026, after a second, separately coded implementation (`sdk/tests/interop/flow-reader.ts`) passed the fixtures. Step 2 of the [verb-layer build order](../../../docs/foundation/verb-layer-proposal.md) (decisions V7, V8, V14). A host admits the exact contract in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json) (publisher `dtp`, semantics `flow-v1`) with `profile.admit`.

## Name and version

Kind `dtp/flow@1`; profile `dtp.flow/1`; contract version `1.0.0`. Minor versions within the major MUST be additive. It does not supersede `dtp/inventory@2`; both may be admitted side by side.

## Nature

An **event** kind whose reducer maintains a **state**: a company's *book*, a set of conserved ledgers under one resource policy. It carries three verbs:

- `move`: goods or money change place, or status, under the same owner.
- `transform`: inputs become outputs.
- `transfer`: title or custody changes hands.

A fact is immutable and is never superseded; corrections are further facts. The book is derived and is not itself a record.

## Identity

- A **book** is the fact's `resource_id`. It is opened under one policy with `flow.open {policy_id, book_id}`, and every fact in it is written under that policy. A fact never spans two books.
- A **ledger** is opened in a book with `flow.ledger.open {policy_id, book_id, asset, account, balance}`. There are two kinds:
  - **Goods ledger:** one product, anchored by its `dtp/product@1` head, whose base unit and tracking it copies. Keyed by SHA-256 of `["product", root]`.
  - **Money ledger:** one currency in one account, anchored by an active `dtp/object@1` of class `dtp/account` the company holds. Keyed by SHA-256 of `["money", currency, account root]`. A money ledger opened with `balance: "signed"` may go negative (a credit line, payables); every other ledger may not.
- Both openings are signed commands that travel with migration.
- A **position** is `{lot_id, location, status, owner}`:
  - `location` is exactly one of `ref` (an [object reference](../verbs.md#object-references): the company's own location, or another company's) and `virtual`.
  - `owner` is a party reference, or null for the company itself.
  - Positions are keyed by SHA-256 of the canonical tuple.
- Each fact is its own record (`root_id = record_id`).

## Payload

Schema in the bounded `dtp.schema/1` dialect with `nullable`, in [`1/profile.json`](1/profile.json):

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `act` | The [act header](../verbs.md#the-act-header); `verb` is `move`, `transform` or `transfer`. |
| `ledgers` | 0 to 16 `{key, expected_revision, expected_digest}`: exactly the ledgers the fact changes, each at its current revision and head digest (null before its first fact). |
| `legs` | 0 to 16 `{asset, from, to, quantity, serial_ids, reason}`. `asset` is exactly one of `product` (a root) and `currency` (a code). |
| `reservations` | 0 to 16 `{kind: reserve \| release \| fulfill, reservation_id, asset, position, quantity, party}`, goods only. |
| `transform` | `{recipe, yield_loss, equipment}` on a transform, otherwise null. |
| `rights` | On a transfer, the rights it moves: `title`, `custody` or both. Otherwise null. |
| `settles` | 0 to 16 `{claim, kind: payment \| offset \| reversal, amount, currency, reverses}`. |

A goods quantity uses the `inventory@2` unit object: the product's base unit, or a published packaging revision, three places, never rounded. A money quantity is `{system: "currency", code}` in the ledger's currency, six places. Nothing is ever converted.

## Semantics (`flow-v1`)

1. **Double entry per asset.** Every leg moves a quantity of one asset between two positions, at least one real. A leg's real positions belong to the asset's ledgers:
   - goods to the product's ledger;
   - money to the ledger of the account the position is in, so one leg can move money between two account ledgers.

   Virtual counterparts:
   - **Goods:** `~supplier`, `~customer`, `~adjustment` (with a reason) and `~production`.
   - **Money:** `~counterparty` and `~adjustment`.

   A virtual position has no lot, no owner, and status `available`.
2. **Never negative, never reserved twice.** A leg from a real position needs that much unreserved quantity there, except in a signed money ledger. Money positions are the company's own account, by root, with no lot and no owner.
3. **Verbs.**
   - **Transform:** carries only production legs, at least one input to `~production` and one output from it, all in one fact and so atomic across ledgers. Every yield loss is of an input product; its amount is informative.
   - **Move:** never changes an owner, and money never meets `~counterparty`.
   - **Transfer:** states `rights`, and its legs change exactly those:
     - **Title** is a change of `owner`.
     - **Custody** is a change of location between two real positions with the same lot and status.
     - **Money** changes hands only by title, with `~counterparty`.
     - Goods enter and leave the book by moves.
     - A transfer with no legs carries an offset or a reversal.
4. **Settlements.** Recorded with the fact for `dtp/claim@1` to interpret:
   - A payment needs money legs in its currency.
   - An offset nets at least two claims.
   - A reversal names the settlement fact it reverses.
5. **Ledgers.** `ledgers` names exactly the ledgers the fact changes. Each must be at its expected revision and head, or the fact is a `revision_conflict`. An accepted fact advances each of them by one revision and sets each one's head to the SHA-256 of the canonical fact: a digest chain per ledger (V14).
6. **Observations.** Per source, per book: the `inventory@2` high-water mark with a 64-sequence window.
7. **Serials and reservations.** As in `inventory@2`. A serialized product also needs a lot.
8. Refusal codes are normative: `invalid`, `wrong_scope`, `observation_conflict`, `stale_observation`, `revision_conflict`, `unit_mismatch`, `tracking_mismatch`, `insufficient_stock`, `insufficient_reservation`, `serial_conflict`, `rights_mismatch`, `ledger_unavailable`.

A host additionally:

- refuses references that spell the writer's own objects with its own organization id (`invalid_flow`);
- verifies that every packaging unit is a revision its product published (`unknown_packaging`);
- writes facts only under the book's policy;
- keeps a fact index for idempotent retries.

On snapshot import a host replays every opening and fact, refuses a fact that predates the opening of a ledger it changes, and must reproduce every book exactly.

## Relations

- `dtp/inventory@2`: a flow goods ledger restricted to the company's own positions is an `inventory@2` ledger. `projectInventory2(book, product)` is the bridge: a derived, marked view in which a location is spelled `root` or `minted_by/root`. It cannot be turned back into a book.
- `dtp/product@1` anchors goods ledgers; `dtp/object@1` (`dtp/account`, `dtp/location`) anchors money ledgers and names locations.
- `dtp/claim@1` (step 4) reads `settles`. Custody is checked structurally here (the location changed). Who operates a location, and so who holds custody, is the `operates` assignment of `dtp/assign@1` (step 3).

Regenerate with `node sdk/scripts/build-flow-profile.ts`; `--check` verifies. The reference implementation is `sdk/src/profiles/flow.ts`: portable, dependency-free, and reachable as `flow` from both preview entries.

## Not in scope

The following are all left out:

- risk of loss (V9);
- valuation (`value@1`);
- containment and handling units (a later logistics kind);
- conversions between units or currencies;
- facts spanning books;
- any claim that a position reflects physical reality.

A book is what a company's producers said happened, checked for consistency.
